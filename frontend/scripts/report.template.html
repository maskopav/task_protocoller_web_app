Spectral verification

Aliasing Audit

Every voice recording gets downsampled from the device's native rate to 44.1 kHz before upload (resampleAudio.js), to cut data usage without touching audio quality. The three tests below run the actual production resampler — not a reimplementation — against standard DSP test signals and inspect the resulting spectra, which is the only reliable way to confirm a downsampler is filtering correctly rather than aliasing.

Test 1 — Noise floor

Frequency response under white noise

White noise carries equal energy at every frequency at once, so feeding it through the resampler and comparing the spectrum before/after directly traces out the filter's measured response — where it stays flat, and where it cuts. A correct anti-aliasing filter stays flat through the audible range and then drops sharply right at the new Nyquist frequency (22.05 kHz); a naive decimator would instead alias the upper band straight back down into the passband.

Test 2 — Swept sine

20 Hz → 24 kHz chirp, before and after

A linear sweep through every frequency at once is the standard textbook check for aliasing: on a spectrogram it draws a single clean diagonal line. If a downsampler folds high frequencies back into the passband instead of filtering them out, a second, mirrored line appears — reflecting back down from the new Nyquist as the sweep approaches it. A correct resampler instead shows the line simply fade to silence right at the boundary.

Test 3 — Multi-tone probe

1 / 8 / 20 / 23.5 kHz test tones

Four pure tones, one of them (23.5 kHz) deliberately placed above the new Nyquist frequency where it cannot survive resampling. The three in-band tones should come through essentially unchanged; the fourth should vanish — with no new peak appearing anywhere else in the spectrum, which is what an aliasing artifact would look like.

Generated by scripts/verifyResampleSpectrum.mjs, which runs the real resampleTo44100() from src/utils/resampleAudio.js (libsamplerate, WASM, SRC_SINC_BEST_QUALITY) against synthetic test signals and writes their spectra to scripts/spectrum-data.json for this page to render. Re-run the script any time the resampler changes to regenerate the data this report is built from.
